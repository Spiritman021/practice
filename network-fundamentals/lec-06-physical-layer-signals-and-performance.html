Section 01

Physical Layer Goal & Signal Classification

The primary functionality of the Physical Layer (Layer 1) is to convert data into electromagnetic signals and transmit them across physical media (wires, fiber, or air).

Concept 1
📊 Signals
Electromagnetic representations of data. Data must be converted into signals before transmission over a medium.
Concept 2
🔄 Periodic Signals
Completes a pattern within a measurable time frame called a period ($T$) and repeats that pattern over subsequent identical periods. A full pattern is a cycle.
Concept 3
📉 Nonperiodic Signals
Changes over time without exhibiting a repeating pattern or cycle. Used to represent digital data.

Section 02

Analog vs Digital Data & Signals

Feature Analog Signal Digital Signal
Value States Continuous; infinite number of values in a range. Discrete; limited number of values (e.g., 0 and 1).
Representation Smooth, continuous sine wave curves. Square wave pulses (discrete voltage levels).
Noise Vulnerability High; noise distorts the signal and is hard to remove. Low; immune to noise, easily regenerated by repeaters.
Unit of Measure Frequency ($f$ in Hz), Period ($T$ in sec). Bit Rate ($N$ in bps), Bit Duration ($\text{sec}$).

Section 03

Sine Wave Parameters

🔥 IBPS SO IT – Fundamental Formula Area

A simple analog signal is represented by a Sine Wave, defined by 3 key parameters: Peak Amplitude ($A$), Frequency ($f$), and Phase ($\phi$).

⚡ Period & Frequency Relationship
$$f = \frac{1}{T} \quad \text{and} \quad T = \frac{1}{f}$$
$T$ = Period in seconds ($\text{s}$)  |  $f$ = Frequency in Hertz ($\text{Hz}$)
Parameter 1
Peak Amplitude ($A$)
Absolute value of highest signal intensity/energy (measured in Volts, Amperes, or Watts).
Parameter 2
Phase ($\phi$)
Describes position of waveform relative to time $t=0$ (measured in degrees or radians). $90^\circ = \pi/2$, $180^\circ = \pi$.
Parameter 3
Wavelength ($\lambda$)
Distance a signal travels in one period: $\lambda = v \times T = \frac{v}{f}$ (where $v = \text{propagation speed}$).

Units of Period and Frequency (Table 3.1)

Period Unit Equivalent in Seconds Frequency Unit Equivalent in Hertz
Seconds (s)$1\text{ s}$Hertz (Hz)$1\text{ Hz}$
Milliseconds (ms)$10^{-3}\text{ s}$Kilohertz (kHz)$10^3\text{ Hz}$
Microseconds ($\mu\text{s}$)$10^{-6}\text{ s}$Megahertz (MHz)$10^6\text{ Hz}$
Nanoseconds (ns)$10^{-9}\text{ s}$Gigahertz (GHz)$10^9\text{ Hz}$
Picoseconds (ps)$10^{-12}\text{ s}$Terahertz (THz)$10^{12}\text{ Hz}$

Section 04

Transmission Impairments (Attenuation, SNR & Decibels)

As a signal travels through a medium, it suffers 3 main types of transmission impairments: Attenuation, Distortion, and Noise.

Impairment 1
🔻 Attenuation
Loss of signal energy due to resistance of medium. Amplifiers/Repeaters are used to restore signal strength. Measured in Decibels ($\text{dB}$).
Impairment 2
🌀 Distortion
Signal changes shape or form in composite signals because different frequency components travel at different speeds (delay distortion).
Impairment 3
⚡ Noise
Random unwanted signals (thermal noise, crosstalk, impulse noise) added to the signal from external sources.

Decibels & Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) Formulas

⚡ Attenuation in Decibels ($\text{dB}$)
$$\text{dB} = 10 \log_{10} \left( \frac{P_2}{P_1} \right)$$
$P_1$ = Input power  |  $P_2$ = Output power ($+\text{dB}$ = amplification, $-\text{dB}$ = attenuation)
⚡ Signal-to-Noise Ratio ($\text{SNR}$)
$$\text{SNR} = \frac{\text{Average Signal Power}}{\text{Average Noise Power}}$$
$$\text{SNR}_{\text{dB}} = 10 \log_{10}(\text{SNR})$$
High $\text{SNR}$ means high signal quality and low noise.

Section 05

Data Rate Limits (Nyquist & Shannon)

🔥 IBPS SO IT – Top Numerical Category
Formula 1 · Noiseless Channel
📐 Nyquist Bit Rate
Calculates theoretical maximum bit rate for a noiseless channel.
$$\text{Bit Rate} = 2 \times B \times \log_2(V)$$
- $B$ = Bandwidth ($\text{Hz}$)
- $V$ = Number of discrete signal levels
Formula 2 · Noisy Channel
📊 Shannon Channel Capacity
Calculates theoretical upper limit capacity ($C$) for a noisy channel.
$$C = B \times \log_2(1 + \text{SNR})$$
- $B$ = Bandwidth ($\text{Hz}$)
- $\text{SNR}$ = Numerical Signal-to-Noise Ratio (NOT in dB!)
⚠️
Crucial Exam Trap for Shannon Capacity
If the question gives $\text{SNR}_{\text{dB}} = 30\text{ dB}$, you MUST convert it to numerical $\text{SNR}$ first before using Shannon formula!
$$\text{SNR}_{\text{dB}} = 10 \log_{10}(\text{SNR}) = 30 \implies \text{SNR} = 10^3 = 1000$$ Then $C = B \log_2(1 + 1000) \approx B \times 10$.
⏱️
Section 06

Network Performance Metrics

Metric 1
Throughput
The actual measurement of how fast data is passed through a network node (always $\le$ Bandwidth).
Metric 2
Latency (Delay)
Total time for a complete message to reach destination:
$$\text{Latency} = T_{\text{propagation}} + T_{\text{transmission}} + T_{\text{queueing}} + T_{\text{processing}}$$
Metric 3
Bandwidth-Delay Product (BDP)
Volume of bits that can fill the transmission pipe:
$$\text{BDP} = \text{Bandwidth (bps)} \times \text{Delay (sec)}$$

Section 08

IBPS SO IT PYQ Practice Questions

PYQ 01 Period & Frequency
A periodic signal has a period $T = 100\ \mu\text{s}$. What is its frequency in kHz?
  • A. $1\text{ kHz}$
  • B. $10\text{ kHz}$ ✓
  • C. $100\text{ kHz}$
  • D. $0.1\text{ kHz}$
Answer: B — 10 kHz
$T = 100\ \mu\text{s} = 100 \times 10^{-6}\text{ s} = 10^{-4}\text{ s}$.
$f = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{1}{10^{-4}} = 10,000\text{ Hz} = \mathbf{10\text{ kHz}}$.
PYQ 02 Nyquist Bit Rate Calculation
Calculate the maximum bit rate for a noiseless channel with a bandwidth of 3 kHz transmitting a signal with 4 signal levels ($V=4$).
  • A. 6 kbps
  • B. 12 kbps ✓
  • C. 24 kbps
  • D. 3 kbps
Answer: B — 12 kbps
Nyquist Formula: $\text{Bit Rate} = 2 \times B \times \log_2(V)$.
$\text{Bit Rate} = 2 \times 3000 \times \log_2(4) = 6000 \times 2 = 12,000\text{ bps} = \mathbf{12\text{ kbps}}$.
PYQ 03 Shannon Channel Capacity
A telephone line has a bandwidth of 3 kHz and a Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) of 1023. What is the theoretical capacity of the channel?
  • A. 15 kbps
  • B. 30 kbps ✓
  • C. 60 kbps
  • D. 3 kbps
Answer: B — 30 kbps
Shannon Formula: $C = B \times \log_2(1 + \text{SNR})$.
$C = 3000 \times \log_2(1 + 1023) = 3000 \times \log_2(1024) = 3000 \times 10 = 30,000\text{ bps} = \mathbf{30\text{ kbps}}$.
PYQ 04 Decibel Attenuation Calculation
If the power of a signal is reduced to half its original value ($P_2 = P_1 / 2$), what is the attenuation in decibels?
  • A. $-3\text{ dB}$ ✓
  • B. $-6\text{ dB}$
  • C. $-10\text{ dB}$
  • D. $-1\text{ dB}$
Answer: A — $-3\text{ dB}$
$\text{dB} = 10 \log_{10}(P_2 / P_1) = 10 \log_{10}(0.5) = 10 \times (-0.301) \approx \mathbf{-3\text{ dB}}$.
PYQ 05 Phase Shift
A sine wave is offset by $\frac{1}{4}$ cycle with respect to time zero ($t=0$). What is its phase in degrees?
  • A. $0^\circ$
  • B. $90^\circ$ ✓
  • C. $180^\circ$
  • D. $360^\circ$
Answer: B — $90^\circ$
One full cycle = $360^\circ$. An offset of $\frac{1}{4}$ cycle = $\frac{1}{4} \times 360^\circ = \mathbf{90^\circ}$ ($\pi/2\text{ radians}$).
PYQ 06 Bandwidth-Delay Product (BDP)
A link has a bandwidth of 100 Mbps and a Round Trip Time (RTT) of 50 ms. Calculate the Bandwidth-Delay Product (BDP) in Bytes.
  • A. 5,000,000 Bytes
  • B. 625,000 Bytes (625 KB) ✓
  • C. 500,000 Bytes
  • D. 1,250,000 Bytes
Answer: B — 625,000 Bytes
$\text{BDP in bits} = \text{Bandwidth} \times \text{Delay} = (100 \times 10^6\text{ bps}) \times (0.05\text{ sec}) = 5,000,000\text{ bits}$.
$\text{BDP in Bytes} = \frac{5,000,000}{8} = \mathbf{625,000\text{ Bytes}}$ ($625\text{ KB}$).
PYQ 07 Wavelength Formula
A signal has a frequency of $2\text{ GHz}$ propagating through free space ($v = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$). What is its wavelength ($\lambda$)?
  • A. $0.15\text{ m}$ ($15\text{ cm}$) ✓
  • B. $1.5\text{ m}$
  • C. $0.015\text{ m}$
  • D. $15\text{ m}$
Answer: A — 0.15 m (15 cm)
$\lambda = \frac{v}{f} = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{2 \times 10^9} = \frac{3}{20} = \mathbf{0.15\text{ meters}} = 15\text{ cm}$.
PYQ 08 SNR in Decibels
If a signal has an average power of $1000\text{ W}$ and noise power is $1\text{ W}$, what is the Signal-to-Noise Ratio in decibels ($\text{SNR}_{\text{dB}}$)?
  • A. $1000\text{ dB}$
  • B. $30\text{ dB}$ ✓
  • C. $10\text{ dB}$
  • D. $3\text{ dB}$
Answer: B — 30 dB
Numerical $\text{SNR} = \frac{1000}{1} = 1000$.
$\text{SNR}_{\text{dB}} = 10 \log_{10}(1000) = 10 \times 3 = \mathbf{30\text{ dB}}$.
PYQ 09 Frequency Domain
A frequency domain plot shows:
  • A. Phase versus Time
  • B. Peak Amplitude versus Frequency ✓
  • C. Wavelength versus Distance
  • D. Bit Rate versus Bandwidth
Answer: B — Peak Amplitude versus Frequency
A time domain plot shows amplitude versus time. A frequency domain plot shows peak amplitude versus frequency.
PYQ 10 Infinite vs Zero Frequency
If a signal does not change at all over time (constant DC voltage), its frequency is:
  • A. 0 Hz ✓
  • B. Infinite Hz
  • C. 1 Hz
  • D. Undefined
Answer: A — 0 Hz
If a signal does not change at all over time, its frequency is $0\text{ Hz}$. If a signal changes instantaneously, its frequency is $\infty\text{ Hz}$.
