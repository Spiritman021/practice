Section 01

Why Analog-to-Analog Modulation?

Analog-to-analog modulation is used when a low-frequency analog signal (such as human voice or audio) needs to be transmitted over a high-frequency bandpass channel (such as free space/air radio channels).

Modulation 1
📻 AM (Amplitude Modulation)
Modifies carrier amplitude. Simple, requires low bandwidth ($2f_m$).
Modulation 2
📻 FM (Frequency Modulation)
Modifies carrier frequency ($f_c \pm \Delta f$). Excellent noise immunity, higher bandwidth ($10f_m$).
Modulation 3
📻 PM (Phase Modulation)
Modifies carrier phase proportional to derivative of modulating amplitude.
📈
Section 02

Amplitude Modulation (AM)

🔥 IBPS SO IT – Core Bandwidth Calculation Topic

In AM, the high-frequency carrier wave's amplitude is varied proportional to the modulating audio signal, forming an envelope.

⚡ AM Bandwidth Formula
$$B_{\text{AM}} = 2 \times B_{\text{signal}} = 2 \times f_m$$
$f_m$ = maximum frequency of the modulating audio signal
📝
AM Radio Station Worked Example
- Audio signal bandwidth $f_m = 5\text{ kHz}$.
- Required total AM station bandwidth $B_{\text{AM}} = 2 \times 5\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{10\text{ kHz}}$.
- AM Band Allocation: $530\text{ kHz}$ to $1700\text{ kHz}$ (each station allocated $10\text{ kHz}$).
🔊
Section 03

Frequency Modulation (FM)

In FM, the frequency of the carrier signal is varied in proportion to the modulating audio signal amplitude. Carrier amplitude remains strictly constant!

⚡ FM Bandwidth Formula (Carson's Rule)
$$B_{\text{FM}} = 2(1 + \beta) f_m \approx 10 \times f_m$$
$\beta = \Delta f / f_m$ = modulation index  |  Requires $\approx 10\times$ signal frequency!
📝
FM Radio Station Worked Example
- Stereo audio signal bandwidth $f_m = 20\text{ kHz}$.
- Required total FM station bandwidth $B_{\text{FM}} = 10 \times 20\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{200\text{ kHz}} = \mathbf{0.2\text{ MHz}}$.
- FM Band Allocation: $88\text{ MHz}$ to $108\text{ MHz}$ (each station allocated $200\text{ kHz}$).
🌀
Section 04

Phase Modulation (PM)

In PM, the phase of the carrier wave is varied proportional to the modulating signal voltage. Phase changes manifest as frequency shifts proportional to the instantaneous derivative of modulating amplitude.

⚡ PM Bandwidth Formula
$$B_{\text{PM}} = 2(1 + \beta) f_m$$
Similar high bandwidth requirement to FM.
🔀
Section 05

Multiplexing Overview (FDM, WDM, TDM)

Multiplexing is the set of techniques that allows the simultaneous transmission of multiple signals across a single data link.

Multiplexing Technique Domain Type Division Criterion Primary Media & Application
FDM (Frequency Division) Analog Bandwidth split into non-overlapping frequency bands. AM/FM radio, Cable TV, ADSL modems.
WDM (Wavelength Division) Analog (Optical) Light combined into different wavelengths (colors). Fiber Optic high-speed backbones (prisms).
TDM (Time Division) Digital Time divided into discrete time slots per channel. T-1 / E-1 digital telephony trunks, LANs.
🛡️
Section 06

FDM & Guard Bands Deep Dive

Concept 1
🛡️ Guard Bands
Small, unused frequency buffers left between adjacent channels in FDM to prevent signal overlap and crosstalk interference.
Concept 2
⏱️ Synchronous vs Statistical TDM
- Synchronous TDM: Fixed time slot pre-allocated to each device (wastes bandwidth if idle).
- Statistical TDM: Time slots allocated dynamically on-demand to active devices.

Section 08

IBPS SO IT PYQ Practice Questions

PYQ 01 AM Bandwidth Calculation
If an audio signal has a maximum frequency of $4\text{ kHz}$, what is the required channel bandwidth for an Amplitude Modulated (AM) transmission?
  • A. 4 kHz
  • B. 8 kHz ✓
  • C. 16 kHz
  • D. 40 kHz
Answer: B — 8 kHz
$B_{\text{AM}} = 2 \times f_m = 2 \times 4\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{8\text{ kHz}}$.
PYQ 02 FM Channel Bandwidth
Each commercial FM radio station broadcast channel in the $88\text{--}108\text{ MHz}$ band is allocated a total bandwidth of:
  • A. 10 kHz
  • B. 200 kHz (0.2 MHz) ✓
  • C. 1 MHz
  • D. 50 kHz
Answer: B — 200 kHz (0.2 MHz)
FM radio stations require approximately $10 \times f_m = 10 \times 20\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{200\text{ kHz}}$ ($0.2\text{ MHz}$) per station.
PYQ 03 FDM Guard Bands
In Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM), what is the purpose of placing "Guard Bands" between adjacent channels?
  • A. To amplify signals
  • B. To prevent channel overlap and crosstalk interference ✓
  • C. To convert analog to digital
  • D. To store frame data
Answer: B
Guard bands are narrow unused frequency ranges inserted between FDM channels to prevent overlap and inter-channel crosstalk.
PYQ 04 WDM Light Medium
Wavelength Division Multiplexing (WDM) is an analog multiplexing technique designed specifically for which medium?
  • A. UTP Cable
  • B. Coaxial Cable
  • C. Fiber Optic Cable ✓
  • D. Satellite Dishes
Answer: C — Fiber Optic Cable
WDM combines multiple light beam signals of different wavelengths (colors) into a single fiber optic cable using optical prisms.
PYQ 05 AM Commercial Frequency Band
Commercial AM radio broadcasting operates within which frequency spectrum band?
  • A. 530 kHz to 1700 kHz (MF Band) ✓
  • B. 88 MHz to 108 MHz (VHF Band)
  • C. 3 GHz to 30 GHz (SHF Band)
  • D. 3 kHz to 30 kHz (VLF Band)
Answer: A — 530 kHz to 1700 kHz
AM radio operates in the Medium Frequency (MF) band from $530\text{--}1700\text{ kHz}$. FM radio operates from $88\text{--}108\text{ MHz}$.
PYQ 06 FM Noise Immunity
Frequency Modulation (FM) is superior to Amplitude Modulation (AM) in sound quality primarily because:
  • A. FM has superior immunity to electrical noise spikes ✓
  • B. FM requires less bandwidth than AM
  • C. FM uses lower carrier frequencies
  • D. FM signals travel through walls while AM cannot
Answer: A
Most natural electrical noise affects signal amplitude. Since FM keeps carrier amplitude constant and encodes data in frequency variations, it rejects amplitude noise effectively.
PYQ 07 Statistical TDM Efficiency
Why is Statistical (Asynchronous) TDM more bandwidth-efficient than Synchronous TDM?
  • A. Statistical TDM uses optical prisms
  • B. Time slots are allocated dynamically only to active lines rather than reserving slots for idle lines ✓
  • C. Statistical TDM does not use time slots
  • D. Synchronous TDM uses guard bands
Answer: B
Synchronous TDM reserves a fixed slot for every channel (wasting slots if idle). Statistical TDM allocates slots dynamically on-demand only when a line has data to send.
PYQ 08 AM Envelope Concept
In Amplitude Modulation (AM), the shape outlining the peaks of the high-frequency carrier wave is called the:
  • A. Envelope ✓
  • B. Sideband
  • C. Guard Band
  • D. Constellation
Answer: A — Envelope
The modulating signal forms the outer **envelope** of the carrier wave in AM.
PYQ 09 FDM Division Parameter
In Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM), multiple signals share a single medium by dividing its:
  • A. Transmission Time
  • B. Available Frequency Bandwidth ✓
  • C. Phase Angles
  • D. Voltage Levels
Answer: B — Frequency Bandwidth
FDM divides the total frequency spectrum into distinct non-overlapping frequency bands.
PYQ 10 Carson's Rule Ratio
According to Carson's Rule, the total bandwidth of an FM signal is approximately how many times the bandwidth of the modulating audio signal?
  • A. 2 times
  • B. 5 times
  • C. 10 times ✓
  • D. 100 times
Answer: C — 10 times
$B_{\text{FM}} \approx 10 \times f_m$. (e.g., $20\text{ kHz}$ audio requires $200\text{ kHz}$ FM bandwidth).
