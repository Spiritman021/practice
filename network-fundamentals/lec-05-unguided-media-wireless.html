Section 01

What is Unguided (Wireless) Media?

Unguided media transports electromagnetic waves without using a physical conductor (wire/cable). Data is transmitted through air, vacuum, or free space.

Spectrum Division 1
📻 Radio Waves ($3\text{ kHz}$ – $1\text{ GHz}$)
Omnidirectional signals that travel in all directions. Penetrate walls easily. Used for AM/FM radio, paging, television.
Spectrum Division 2
📡 Microwaves ($1\text{ GHz}$ – $300\text{ GHz}$)
Directional signals (line-of-sight). Cannot penetrate solid walls. Used for cellular phones, satellite links, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth.
Spectrum Division 3
🔴 Infrared ($300\text{ GHz}$ – $400\text{ THz}$)
High frequency, short range. Cannot penetrate walls (prevents interference between rooms). Used in TV remotes, IrDA.

Section 02

Three Wireless Propagation Methods

🔥 IBPS SO IT – High Frequency Exam Topic
🏞️ Ground Propagation
Frequency: Below $2\text{ MHz}$
- Radio waves travel through the lowest atmosphere, hugging the curvature of the Earth.
- Signals radiate in all directions.
- Distance depends on signal power.
- Example: VLF / LF radio navigation, submarine communications.
🌌 Sky Propagation
Frequency: $2\text{ MHz}$ to $30\text{ MHz}$
- Higher frequency radio waves radiate upward and bounce (reflect) off the Ionosphere layer of the atmosphere back to Earth.
- Allows long-distance intercontinental communication with lower power.
- Example: Shortwave radio, Citizens Band (CB) radio, International broadcasting (BBC World Service).
📐 Line-of-Sight (LOS)
Frequency: Above $30\text{ MHz}$
- Very high frequency signals travel in straight line paths directly from transmitter to receiver.
- Antennas must be elevated and precisely aligned.
- Waves cannot bend around Earth or penetrate heavy obstacles.
- Example: Satellite communication, Cellular networks, 5G, Radar.

Section 04

Radio Waves vs. Microwaves vs. Infrared

Category 1
📻 Radio Waves
- Frequency: $3\text{ kHz}$ to $1\text{ GHz}$.
- Antenna: Omnidirectional (propagates in all directions).
- Wall Penetration: Can penetrate walls (ideal for indoor reception).
- Use: AM/FM radio, television, cordless phones.
Category 2
📡 Microwaves
- Frequency: $1\text{ GHz}$ to $300\text{ GHz}$.
- Antenna: Directional (parabolic dish / horn antenna).
- Wall Penetration: Cannot penetrate solid walls.
- Use: Cellular networks, Satellite links, Wi-Fi ($2.4\text{ GHz} / 5\text{ GHz}$), Bluetooth.
Category 3
🔴 Infrared Signals
- Frequency: $300\text{ GHz}$ to $400\text{ THz}$.
- Propagation: Short-range Line-of-sight in closed areas.
- Wall Penetration: Cannot penetrate walls (prevents interference between rooms).
- Use: TV remote controls, IrDA, short-range device sensors.

Section 05

Satellite Communication & Orbits

Satellite communication uses microwaves (SHF/EHF bands) to relay signals between ground earth stations across vast global distances. The satellite acts as a **transponder** (amplifies and changes frequency of received signal before retransmitting).

:// 6 – 12 hours
Orbit Type Acronym Altitude / Height Orbital Period Primary Application
Geostationary Earth Orbit GEO $35,786\text{ km}$ ($36,000\text{ km}$) 24 hours (Matches Earth rotation) TV broadcasting, weather satellites, fixed communications (3 GEO satellites cover entire globe).
Medium Earth Orbit MEO $5,000\text{--}15,000\text{ km}$ Global Positioning System (GPS, 24 satellites at ~$20,200\text{ km}$).
Low Earth Orbit LEO $500\text{--}2,000\text{ km}$ 90 – 120 minutes High-speed low-latency satellite internet (Starlink, OneWeb), Iridium satellite phone.

Section 07

IBPS SO IT PYQ Practice Questions

PYQ 01 Sky Propagation
In Sky Propagation, radio waves in the frequency range of 2 MHz to 30 MHz are reflected back to Earth by which layer of the atmosphere?
  • A. Troposphere
  • B. Stratosphere
  • C. Ionosphere ✓
  • D. Exosphere
Answer: C — Ionosphere
Sky wave propagation relies on ionized particles in the Ionosphere layer to reflect high-frequency (HF) signals back to Earth, enabling long-distance shortwave communications.
PYQ 02 AM Radio Band
AM Radio broadcasting operates primarily in which frequency band?
  • A. VLF
  • B. LF
  • C. MF (Middle Frequency) ✓
  • D. VHF
Answer: C — MF (Middle Frequency)
MF band ($300\text{ kHz}$ to $3\text{ MHz}$) is standard for AM radio broadcasting. FM radio operates in the VHF band ($30\text{--}300\text{ MHz}$).
PYQ 03 Infrared Wall Penetration
Why do infrared TV remote controls not interfere with televisions operating in adjacent rooms?
  • A. Infrared waves are omnidirectional
  • B. Infrared waves cannot penetrate solid walls ✓
  • C. Infrared waves use ground propagation
  • D. Infrared waves travel in sky propagation
Answer: B
High-frequency infrared signals ($300\text{ GHz}$ – $400\text{ THz}$) cannot pass through solid walls, confining the signal strictly within a single room.
PYQ 04 Cellular & Wi-Fi Frequency Band
Cellular mobile phones, Wi-Fi ($2.4\text{ GHz}$), and Bluetooth operate in which frequency band?
  • A. VHF
  • B. UHF (Ultra High Frequency) ✓
  • C. SHF
  • D. MF
Answer: B — UHF (Ultra High Frequency)
UHF band ($300\text{ MHz}$ to $3\text{ GHz}$) hosts UHF TV, Cellular mobile communications, Wi-Fi ($2.4\text{ GHz}$), and Bluetooth.
PYQ 05 GEO Satellite Height
What is the approximate altitude of a Geostationary Earth Orbit (GEO) satellite above the equator?
  • A. $1,000\text{ km}$
  • B. $10,000\text{ km}$
  • C. $35,786\text{ km}$ (~$36,000\text{ km}$) ✓
  • D. $100,000\text{ km}$
Answer: C — $35,786\text{ km}$
GEO satellites orbit at approximately $35,786\text{ km}$ ($36,000\text{ km}$), completing one orbit in 24 hours to match Earth's rotational speed.
PYQ 06 Ground Propagation Frequency
Ground wave propagation (surface wave) is effective for frequencies:
  • A. Below 2 MHz ✓
  • B. Between 30 MHz and 300 MHz
  • C. Above 1 GHz
  • D. Above 300 GHz
Answer: A — Below 2 MHz
Ground wave propagation follows Earth's curvature at low frequencies (below $2\text{ MHz}$) in VLF and LF bands.
PYQ 07 GPS Satellite Orbit
The Global Positioning System (GPS) satellite constellation operates in which orbit?
  • A. LEO
  • B. MEO (Medium Earth Orbit) ✓
  • C. GEO
  • D. HEO
Answer: B — MEO
GPS satellites operate in Medium Earth Orbit (MEO) at an altitude of approximately $20,200\text{ km}$ with an 12-hour orbital period.
PYQ 08 Omnidirectional vs Directional
Which type of wireless wave uses omnidirectional antennas to broadcast signals in all directions?
  • A. Radio Waves ✓
  • B. Microwaves
  • C. Infrared Waves
  • D. Laser Beams
Answer: A — Radio Waves
Radio waves ($3\text{ kHz}$ to $1\text{ GHz}$) use omnidirectional antennas. Microwaves ($1\text{ GHz}$ to $300\text{ GHz}$) use directional dish/horn antennas.
PYQ 09 Minimum GEO Satellites
What is the minimum number of Geostationary (GEO) satellites required to provide global communication coverage around the Earth?
  • A. 2
  • B. 3 ✓
  • C. 6
  • D. 24
Answer: B — 3
Because each GEO satellite covers approximately $120^\circ$ of longitude, a minimum of 3 GEO satellites spaced $120^\circ$ apart can cover the entire Earth (excluding polar caps).
PYQ 10 Super High Frequency (SHF)
Super High Frequency (SHF) band ($3\text{ GHz}$ to $30\text{ GHz}$) is primarily used for:
  • A. AM Radio
  • B. Submarine navigation
  • C. Satellite communication & Radar ✓
  • D. Shortwave radio
Answer: C — Satellite communication & Radar
SHF ($3\text{--}30\text{ GHz}$) provides line-of-sight propagation used for satellite transponders, radar, and microwave relays.
