Section 01

Classes of Transmission Media

A transmission medium is the physical path between transmitter and receiver in a data transmission system. It is located at the Physical Layer (Layer 1) of the OSI model.

🔌 Guided (Wired) Media
Provides a physical conduit from one device to another.
- Twisted-Pair Cable (UTP / STP)
- Coaxial Cable (RG-58, RG-59, RG-11)
- Fiber-Optic Cable (Single-mode / Multi-mode)
📡 Unguided (Wireless) Media
Transmits electromagnetic waves without using a physical conductor (free space / air).
- Radio Waves (Omnidirectional)
- Microwaves (Line-of-sight directional)
- Infrared Waves (Short range, line-of-sight)

Section 02

Twisted-Pair Cables & UTP Categories

🔥 IBPS SO IT – High Frequency Topic

Consists of two insulated copper wires twisted together in a helical spiral. **Why twist?** Twisting causes electromagnetic noise from external sources or neighboring pairs (crosstalk) to affect both wires equally, canceling out interference!

Type A
UTP (Unshielded Twisted-Pair)
Most common in LANs. Inexpensive, flexible, easy to install. No extra metal shielding.
Type B
STP (Shielded Twisted-Pair)
Includes a metallic foil/braid shield surrounding each pair and/or entire cable. Reduces Crosstalk and EMI significantly.

UTP Categories Table (Forouzan Standards)

Category Data Rate Specification & Typical Usage
CAT 1 < 0.1 Mbps Unshielded twisted-pair used in traditional voice telephone systems.
CAT 2 2 Mbps Used in T-1 carrier lines.
CAT 3 10 Mbps Improved CAT 2 used in 10BASE-T Ethernet LANs (16 MHz).
CAT 4 20 Mbps Used in Token Ring networks (20 MHz).
CAT 5 100 Mbps 24 AWG wire, used in 100BASE-TX Fast Ethernet LANs (100 MHz).
CAT 5e 125 Mbps / 1 Gbps Enhanced CAT 5 with extra features to minimize crosstalk for Gigabit Ethernet.
CAT 6 200 Mbps / 10 Gbps Matched components, tested up to 250 MHz (used in 10GBASE-T up to 55m).
CAT 7 (SSTP) 600 Mbps / 10 Gbps Shielded Screen Twisted-Pair. Each pair individually foil-wrapped + outer braid. Lowest crosstalk.

Section 03

Coaxial Cables & Radio Government (RG) Ratings

Coaxial cable carries signals of higher frequency ranges than twisted-pair cable. It consists of a central solid copper conductor enclosed by an insulating dielectric layer, a metallic outer braid shield, and a protective outer jacket.

Category / Rating Impedance ($\Omega$) Primary Application
RG-59 $75\ \Omega$ Cable TV (CATV) and analog video signals.
RG-58 $50\ \Omega$ Thin Ethernet (10BASE2 Thinnet) – legacy LANs.
RG-11 $50\ \Omega$ Thick Ethernet (10BASE5 Thicknet) – long-distance backbone lines.

Section 04

Fiber Optic Cable & Total Internal Reflection

💡
Total Internal Reflection Principle

Light travels through the inner glass core by bouncing off the outer cladding. For light to remain trapped inside, the core's refractive index $n_1$ must be greater than the cladding's refractive index $n_2$ ($n_1 > n_2$), and the angle of incidence must exceed the critical angle ($\theta > \theta_c$).

Anatomy
Core & Cladding
- Core: Ultra-pure glass/plastic core carrying light.
- Cladding: Glass/plastic wrapping with lower refractive index ($n_2 < n_1$) to reflect light back.
- Buffer & Kevlar: Protective plastic coating & strength fibers.
Key Advantages
Why Use Fiber Optics?
- Immune to EMI / RFI: Light signals don't pick up electrical noise.
- Ultra-High Bandwidth: Gigabits/Terabits per second.
- Low Attenuation: Signal travels tens of kilometers without repeaters.
🌈
Section 05

Fiber Propagation Modes

Mode Core Index Profile Light Path / Characteristics Typical Usage
Multimode Step-Index Constant density from center to boundary; abrupt step change at cladding. Multiple light rays bounce in sharp zig-zag paths. High modal dispersion (distortion at destination). Short distance, low-cost LANs (LED light source).
Multimode Graded-Index Varies smoothly from highest at center to lowest at core-cladding boundary. Light beams travel in smooth parabolic / sinusoidal curves. Reduced modal dispersion. Medium distance LAN backbones.
Single-Mode (SMF) Extremely small core diameter (8–10 $\mu\text{m}$). Step index. Light travels in a single straight ray along core axis. Zero modal dispersion. High speed. Long-distance telecommunications, submarine cables (Laser diode source).

Section 06

Network Connectors Summary

Cable Type Connector Name Key Features & Description
Twisted Pair (UTP/STP) RJ-45 (Registered Jack 45) 8-pin / 8-position plastic modular connector (Male plug & Female jack). Standard for Ethernet.
Telephone Line RJ-11 4-pin / 6-position modular connector used for standard telephone handsets and ADSL modems.
Coaxial Cable BNC Connector Bayonet Neill–Concelman connector. Uses twist-lock mechanism. Includes BNC T-connector & $50\ \Omega$ BNC Terminator.
Fiber Optic (Push-Pull) SC Connector Subscriber Connector / Square Connector. Uses push-pull latching mechanism. Common in datacenter fiber.
Fiber Optic (Bayonet) ST Connector Straight Tip / Stick-and-Twist. Uses a round bayonet twist-lock mount.
Fiber Optic (Duplex) MT-RJ Connector Mechanical Transfer Registered Jack. Small form factor duplex connector housing 2 fibers in one RJ-45 size plug.

Section 07

Unguided (Wireless) Media

Wireless Type 1
📻 Radio Waves ($3\text{ kHz}$ – $1\text{ GHz}$)
- Omnidirectional: Propagate in all directions from sending antenna.
- Can penetrate walls (long wavelength).
- Used for AM/FM radio, cellular phones, television broadcasting.
Wireless Type 2
📡 Microwaves ($1\text{ GHz}$ – $300\text{ GHz}$)
- Unidirectional / Line-of-sight: Sending and receiving antennas must be precisely aligned.
- Cannot penetrate walls.
- Used for satellite communication, dish links, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth.
Wireless Type 3
🔴 Infrared ($300\text{ GHz}$ – $400\text{ THz}$)
- High frequency, short range.
- Cannot penetrate walls or solid objects (prevents interference between adjacent rooms).
- Used in TV remote controls, IrDA, short-range device pairing.

Section 09

IBPS SO IT PYQ Practice Questions

PYQ 01 Ethernet Connector
Which connector is standard for connecting UTP/STP cables in an Ethernet LAN?
  • A. RJ-11
  • B. RJ-45 ✓
  • C. BNC
  • D. ST Connector
Answer: B — RJ-45
RJ-45 (Registered Jack 45) is the 8-pin connector used for Ethernet UTP/STP cables. RJ-11 is for telephone lines (4-pin), and BNC is for coaxial cable.
PYQ 02 Coaxial Ratings
Which Radio Government (RG) rating of coaxial cable is used for Cable TV (CATV)?
  • A. RG-58
  • B. RG-59 ✓
  • C. RG-11
  • D. RG-8
Answer: B — RG-59
RG-59 has an impedance of $75\ \Omega$ and is used for Cable TV. RG-58 ($50\ \Omega$) is for Thin Ethernet (10BASE2), and RG-11 ($50\ \Omega$) is for Thick Ethernet (10BASE5).
PYQ 03 Fiber Propagation Principle
Light signals travel through optical fibers based on which principle of physics?
  • A. Refraction
  • B. Total Internal Reflection ✓
  • C. Dispersion
  • D. Diffraction
Answer: B — Total Internal Reflection
Light is trapped inside the core because the core refractive index $n_1$ is higher than cladding $n_2$, causing total internal reflection when angle of incidence exceeds critical angle.
PYQ 04 Single Mode Fiber
Which fiber optic propagation mode provides the highest bandwidth and longest transmission distance with virtually zero modal dispersion?
  • A. Multimode Step-Index
  • B. Multimode Graded-Index
  • C. Single-Mode Fiber (SMF) ✓
  • D. Dual-Mode Fiber
Answer: C — Single-Mode Fiber (SMF)
Single-mode fiber has a very narrow core (8–10 $\mu\text{m}$) allowing light to travel in a single direct ray, eliminating modal dispersion.
PYQ 05 UTP Category 5e
Which category of UTP cable is rated for data speeds up to 1 Gbps (Gigabit Ethernet)?
  • A. CAT 3
  • B. CAT 4
  • C. CAT 5e ✓
  • D. CAT 1
Answer: C — CAT 5e
CAT 5e (Category 5 Enhanced) supports up to 1000 Mbps / 1 Gbps for Gigabit Ethernet with reduced crosstalk.
PYQ 06 EMI Immunity
Which transmission medium is completely immune to Electromagnetic Interference (EMI) and Radio Frequency Interference (RFI)?
  • A. UTP Cable
  • B. Coaxial Cable
  • C. Optical Fiber Cable ✓
  • D. STP Cable
Answer: C — Optical Fiber Cable
Since optical fibers transmit light (photons) rather than electrical currents, they are 100% immune to EMI and RFI.
PYQ 07 BNC Terminator
In a 10BASE2 Coaxial Bus network, what is attached to both ends of the backbone cable to absorb signals?
  • A. RJ-45 Plug
  • B. $50\ \Omega$ BNC Terminator ✓
  • C. Repeater
  • D. Ground wire only
Answer: B — $50\ \Omega$ BNC Terminator
BNC terminators ($50\ \Omega$) absorb traveling electrical signals at the ends of coaxial bus lines to prevent signal reflection back into the cable.
PYQ 08 Infrared Propagation
Which wireless transmission medium cannot pass through solid walls, making it ideal for short-range indoor remote controls?
  • A. Radio Waves
  • B. Microwaves
  • C. Infrared Waves ✓
  • D. Satellite Waves
Answer: C — Infrared Waves
Infrared frequencies ($300\text{ GHz}$ – $400\text{ THz}$) cannot penetrate solid walls, preventing interference between TV remotes in adjacent rooms.
PYQ 09 Coaxial Cable Impedance
Thin Ethernet (10BASE2) coaxial cable has a characteristic impedance of:
  • A. $50\ \Omega$ ✓
  • B. $75\ \Omega$
  • C. $100\ \Omega$
  • D. $150\ \Omega$
Answer: A — $50\ \Omega$
Thin Ethernet (RG-58) and Thick Ethernet (RG-11) use $50\ \Omega$ coaxial cable. Cable TV (RG-59) uses $75\ \Omega$.
PYQ 10 Layer Mapping
At which layer of the OSI reference model do transmission media (cables, connectors, fiber) operate?
  • A. Physical Layer (Layer 1) ✓
  • B. Data Link Layer (Layer 2)
  • C. Network Layer (Layer 3)
  • D. Transport Layer (Layer 4)
Answer: A — Physical Layer (Layer 1)
Transmission media, cables, connectors, signal encoding, and physical interfaces belong to Layer 1 (Physical Layer).
