Section 01

What is a Modem? Modulation vs. Demodulation

💡
Modem = MOdulator + DEmodulator

A Modem bridges digital computer data and analog transmission lines (like telephone lines or cable coax).

Process 1
📡 Modulation (Sender)
The process of converting a digital signal into an analog signal by modifying the amplitude, frequency, or phase of a high-frequency carrier wave.
Process 2
🔌 Demodulation (Receiver)
The process of recovering the original digital binary data from the received modulated analog carrier wave.

Section 03

Digital-to-Analog Modulation (ASK, FSK, PSK, QAM)

🔥 IBPS SO IT – Core High Frequency Exam Section
Technique 1
ASK (Amplitude Shift Keying)
- Modifies: Carrier amplitude.
- Bit 1 = Presence of carrier signal ($+V$). Bit 0 = $0\text{V}$ (On-Off Keying).
- Bandwidth: $B = (1+d) \times S$.
- Highly susceptible to noise.
Technique 2
FSK (Frequency Shift Keying)
- Modifies: Carrier frequency.
- Bit 1 = Frequency $f_1$. Bit 0 = Frequency $f_2$. Constant amplitude.
- Bandwidth: $B = (1+d)S + 2\Delta f$.
- More noise-immune than ASK.
Technique 3
PSK (Phase Shift Keying)
- Modifies: Carrier phase.
- Bit 1 = Phase $0^\circ$. Bit 0 = Phase $180^\circ$ ($\pi$).
- QPSK (4-PSK): 4 phase angles ($45^\circ, 135^\circ, 225^\circ, 315^\circ$), carries 2 bits/symbol ($r=2$).
- Highly robust against noise.
Technique 4
QAM (Quadrature Amplitude Modulation)
- Modifies: BOTH Amplitude AND Phase simultaneously!
- 4-QAM: $r=2\text{ bits/symbol}$.
- 16-QAM: $r=4\text{ bits/symbol}$.
- 64-QAM: $r=6\text{ bits/symbol}$.
- Highest spectral efficiency (Wi-Fi 6, 5G, Cable modems).

Section 04

Analog-to-Digital Digitization (PCM)

PCM (Pulse Code Modulation) is the standard method for converting an analog voice/audio signal into a digital stream of 0s and 1s.

1
Step 1: Sampling (PAM - Pulse Amplitude Modulation)
Measures the continuous analog signal amplitude at regular time intervals $T_s = 1/f_s$. Converts continuous signal into discrete pulses.
⚡ Nyquist Sampling Theorem
$$f_s \ge 2 \times f_{\max}$$
Sampling rate $f_s$ must be at least twice the maximum signal frequency $f_{\max}$ to prevent aliasing!
2
Step 2: Quantization
Rounds the sample amplitudes into $L$ discrete quantization levels. The difference between original sample and quantized level is Quantization Error / Noise.
3
Step 3: Encoding
Converts each quantized level into an $n$-bit binary word where $n = \log_2(L)$.
$$\text{PCM Bit Rate} = f_s \times n = (2 \times f_{\max}) \times n$$
📝
Standard Telephony Voice PCM Calculation
- Voice bandwidth = $4\text{ kHz}$ ($0$ to $4000\text{ Hz}$).
- Minimum Nyquist Sampling Rate $f_s = 2 \times 4000 = \mathbf{8000\text{ samples/sec}}$.
- If each sample is quantized into 256 levels ($L=256 \implies n = \log_2 256 = 8\text{ bits}$):
$$\text{PCM Voice Bit Rate} = 8000\text{ samples/sec} \times 8\text{ bits/sample} = \mathbf{64,000\text{ bps}} = \mathbf{64\text{ kbps}} \quad (\text{Standard DS0 Channel!})$$
📈
Section 05

Delta Modulation (DM) & PAM

Alternative A/D
📉 Delta Modulation (DM)
Instead of sending full $n$-bit codes for every sample, DM sends only 1 bit per sample!
- If current signal sample is higher than previous staircase estimate $\implies$ send `1`.
- If current signal sample is lower than previous estimate $\implies$ send `0`.
- Significantly reduces hardware complexity and bit rate!
Intermediate Signal
📊 Pulse Amplitude Modulation (PAM)
An intermediate analog-pulse signal produced during the sampling stage of PCM. Pulses have discrete positions but continuous amplitude levels.
📻
Section 06

Analog-to-Analog Modulation (AM, FM, PM)

Modulation Type Carrier Parameter Modified Bandwidth Requirement Primary Application
AM (Amplitude Modulation) Amplitude of high-frequency carrier wave. $$B_{\text{AM}} = 2 \times B_{\text{signal}}$$ AM Radio Broadcasting ($530\text{--}1700\text{ kHz}$).
FM (Frequency Modulation) Frequency of high-frequency carrier wave. $$B_{\text{FM}} = 2(1 + \beta)B_{\text{signal}}$$ FM Radio Broadcasting ($88\text{--}108\text{ MHz}$). Superior noise immunity.
PM (Phase Modulation) Phase of high-frequency carrier wave. $$B_{\text{PM}} = 2(1 + \beta)B_{\text{signal}}$$ Alternative to FM in synthesisers and telemetry.

Section 08

IBPS SO IT PYQ Practice Questions

PYQ 01 Nyquist Sampling Rate
According to the Nyquist Sampling Theorem, what is the minimum sampling rate required to digitize a telephone voice signal with a maximum frequency of $4\text{ kHz}$?
  • A. 4,000 samples/sec
  • B. 8,000 samples/sec ✓
  • C. 16,000 samples/sec
  • D. 2,000 samples/sec
Answer: B — 8,000 samples/sec
Nyquist Sampling Theorem: $f_s \ge 2 \times f_{\max} = 2 \times 4000 = \mathbf{8000\text{ samples/sec}}$.
PYQ 02 PCM Voice Channel Bit Rate
If a voice signal sampled at $8,000\text{ samples/sec}$ uses 256 quantization levels ($L=256$), what is the bit rate of the resulting PCM channel?
  • A. 32 kbps
  • B. 64 kbps ✓
  • C. 128 kbps
  • D. 256 kbps
Answer: B — 64 kbps
$n = \log_2(256) = 8\text{ bits per sample}$.
$\text{Bit Rate} = 8000 \times 8 = 64,000\text{ bps} = \mathbf{64\text{ kbps}}$ (Standard DS0 rate).
PYQ 03 QPSK Bits per Symbol
In QPSK (Quadrature Phase Shift Keying), how many bits are transmitted in each signal symbol element?
  • A. 1 bit
  • B. 2 bits ✓
  • C. 3 bits
  • D. 4 bits
Answer: B — 2 bits
QPSK uses 4 phase angles ($L=4 \implies r = \log_2 4 = \mathbf{2\text{ bits/symbol}}$).
PYQ 04 QAM Combination
Quadrature Amplitude Modulation (QAM) is a digital-to-analog modulation technique that combines changes in:
  • A. Frequency and Period
  • B. Wavelength and Phase
  • C. Amplitude and Phase ✓
  • D. Frequency and Amplitude
Answer: C — Amplitude and Phase
QAM combines Amplitude Shift Keying (ASK) and Phase Shift Keying (PSK) to achieve high bit rates per signal element.
PYQ 05 Delta Modulation Bit Count
Unlike standard PCM which uses multi-bit codes, Delta Modulation (DM) transmits how many bits per sample?
  • A. 1 bit per sample ✓
  • B. 4 bits per sample
  • C. 8 bits per sample
  • D. 16 bits per sample
Answer: A — 1 bit per sample
Delta Modulation (DM) records only the relative change (+1 or -1) from the previous sample, transmitting strictly 1 bit per sample.
PYQ 06 Baud Rate for 16-QAM
If a 16-QAM signal has a bit rate of $9600\text{ bps}$, what is the baud rate of the signal?
  • A. 9600 baud
  • B. 4800 baud
  • C. 2400 baud ✓
  • D. 1200 baud
Answer: C — 2400 baud
16-QAM has $M=16$ states $\implies r = \log_2(16) = 4\text{ bits/symbol}$.
$\text{Baud Rate } S = \frac{N}{r} = \frac{9600}{4} = \mathbf{2400\text{ baud}}$.
PYQ 07 AM Bandwidth Calculation
An audio signal with a maximum frequency of $5\text{ kHz}$ is modulated using Amplitude Modulation (AM). What is the total bandwidth required for the AM signal?
  • A. 5 kHz
  • B. 10 kHz ✓
  • C. 2.5 kHz
  • D. 20 kHz
Answer: B — 10 kHz
Formula for AM Bandwidth: $B_{\text{AM}} = 2 \times B_{\text{signal}} = 2 \times 5\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{10\text{ kHz}}$ (Upper & Lower Sidebands).
PYQ 08 Modem Primary Function
The primary function of a modem at the sending end of a telephone transmission line is:
  • A. Demodulation (Analog to Digital)
  • B. Modulation (Digital to Analog) ✓
  • C. Packet Routing
  • D. Frame Check Sequence generation
Answer: B — Modulation
At the sender side, a modem modulates digital computer signals into analog signals suitable for telephone lines.
PYQ 09 FSK Noise Immunity
Why is Frequency Shift Keying (FSK) less susceptible to amplitude noise interference than Amplitude Shift Keying (ASK)?
  • A. FSK keeps carrier amplitude constant ✓
  • B. FSK uses lower bandwidth
  • C. FSK does not use a carrier wave
  • D. FSK is a baseband technique
Answer: A
Because FSK maintains a constant peak amplitude for both binary 0 and 1 (shifting only frequency), amplitude noise spikes do not easily corrupt the binary decision logic.
PYQ 10 PCM Quantization Error
In Pulse Code Modulation (PCM), increasing the number of quantization levels ($L$) results in:
  • A. Increased quantization noise
  • B. Reduced quantization noise and higher signal accuracy ✓
  • C. Reduced sampling rate
  • D. Lower bit rate
Answer: B
Increasing $L$ makes step sizes smaller, reducing quantization error/noise and improving signal fidelity (at the expense of requiring more bits per sample $n = \log_2 L$).
